The method under the hood

Gamut operates on the ten structural laws of design. Nothing it outputs breaks its own rules.

01Pick a dark anchor before you pick a bright

Bright plus bright is chaos. Bright plus dark is identity. Every loud brand that works has a black, navy, or charcoal grounding the noise.

02Two loud colors can lead together — nothing can lead muted

Primary and Secondary are both allowed to run near full saturation; two confident voices can share a stage. What the law forbids is the other failure: everything muted, nothing leading.

04Design the dark mode at the same time as the light

If your brand color only works on white, you have a website background, not a brand color. Every direction brief ships both light and dark canvas specs.

05Two loud colors is a duet. Three is a circus

Primary and Secondary can both shout. A third loud color doesn't add a voice, it starts a fight neither of them can win.

08Build the ramp, not the swatch

A brand color is not one hex anymore; it is a family. Hover states, tinted surfaces, borders, pressed states. If the color only works at one step, it is a poster, not a system.

09Contrast is a floor, not a vibe

Body text needs 4.5:1 against its background; large text and UI need 3:1. The system enforces the floor numerically on every canvas layout.

Yes. You can lock your primary hex code, and the brief compiler resolves the secondary and canvas tokens around it to satisfy contrast laws.
