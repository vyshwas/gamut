The method under the hood

The Engine is The Brand Color Bible turned into software. Four roles, ten laws, nine psychological signals. Nothing it outputs breaks its own rules.

The laws, applied to your current palette

    01Pick a dark anchor before you pick a bright

    Bright plus bright is chaos. Bright plus dark is identity. Every loud brand that works has a black, navy, or charcoal grounding the noise. The Engine forges an Ink anchor into every palette.

    02Saturate the brand color, mute the accent

    If both colors run at full saturation, they fight. If both are muted, the brand looks tired. One leads at full power; the other supports at 60 to 80 percent. The Engine enforces the ratio numerically — but which hue the accent takes is a separate, looser decision: usually the category's own curated pairing, sometimes a complementary, split-complementary, or triadic hue rolled from the brand color itself. The law fixes the relationship, not the hue.

    03Test at 5% of the screen, not 50%

    Most combos look great as a giant swatch and terrible as a real interface. That is why every generated palette renders into a live landing-page mock at realistic proportions before you export it.

    04Design the dark mode at the same time as the light

    If your brand color only works on white, you have a website background, not a brand color. Every export ships both deployments, contrast-checked against both canvases.

    05One bold color is a brand. Three is a circus

    If you need a third loud color, the first two are not doing their job. The Fixer counts your brights and retires the extras into neutrals.

    06Use color for hierarchy, not decoration

    Color should tell the eye where to go. If you cannot name what each color is doing for the user, the palette is decoration. Every Engine swatch ships with its job written on it.

    07Borrow a palette from a category you are not in

    Beauty colors for a SaaS. Hospitality colors for fintech. Standing out is mostly refusing to look like your category. The Break category toggle does exactly this, deliberately.

    08Build the ramp, not the swatch

    A brand color is not one hex anymore; it is a family. Hover states, tinted surfaces, borders, pressed states, charts. Every swatch here opens into a ten-step tint-to-shade scale the day you pick it. If the color only works at one step, it is a poster, not a system.

    09Contrast is a floor, not a vibe

    Body text needs 4.5:1 against its background; large text and UI need 3:1 — in both deployments. The Engine enforces the floor numerically on every palette it ships, shows you the ratios, and simulates four types of color-blind vision before you commit.

    10Check the culture map before crossing borders

    Your palette ships worldwide by default; red is luck in Shanghai and urgency in Chicago, white is craft in Copenhagen and mourning in Seoul. You rarely change the color — you change the deployment: a 30% hero in one market may need to be a 10% accent in another.

    Questions

    How do I get my license after paying?

    Your license code will be emailed to you within 12 hours of payment. You can redeem it in the Pricing section on any device to activate your plan.

    Can I use it without paying?

    Yes. The Free tier is real and unlimited for palette generation. You can build and export palettes forever without paying.

    Can I keep my existing brand color?

    Yes. Lock any hex in the Engine and it builds the Dominant, Accent, and Ink around it, contrast-checked in both deployments. Your color leads; the system forms around it.

    Do the exports work for print?

    Every swatch carries CMYK values using an uncoated-stock approximation, and Studio plans can print a full brand sheet. For press-critical work, treat them as a proofing starting point and confirm against a calibrated profile.

    What does the print-shift warning on a swatch mean?

    Process inks cannot reproduce every screen color; saturated greens, cyans, and violets dull the most. When a color sits outside typical CMYK range, the swatch flags it and offers a press-safer alternate with the same hue. It is a designer's rule of thumb, not an ICC conversion, so it warns early rather than promising precision.

    Who owns the palettes I generate?

    You do. Everything the Engine or the Fixer produces is yours to use in client and commercial work, on any plan including Free.

    What happens when I cancel?

    Everything you exported stays yours. Your account drops to Free at the end of the billing period; nothing you shipped ever depends on us.
